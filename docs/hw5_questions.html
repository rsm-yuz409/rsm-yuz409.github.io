Introduction

In this exercise, I explored a customer satisfaction survey and compared several approaches for identifying the factors that have the strongest relationship with overall satisfaction.

Rather than relying on a single metric, I examined six different measures of importance:

  • Pearson correlations
  • Standardized regression coefficients
  • Drop-one (“usefulness”) importance
  • Shapley / LMG values
  • Johnson’s relative weights
  • Random forest importance

Each method measures importance from a slightly different perspective. Comparing them side by side provides a more complete view of which perceptions appear to matter most to customers.

Shapley / LMG Values

Shapley values estimate the contribution of each driver by looking at many different combinations of predictors rather than a single model specification.

This approach is useful when several drivers are related to one another because it helps distribute importance more fairly across variables. For that reason, Shapley values are often used as a benchmark measure in key driver analysis.

Conclusion

Across all methods, three drivers consistently appeared near the top: impact, trust, and service. Although the exact rankings varied from one approach to another, these variables remained among the strongest predictors of customer satisfaction.

The Shapley results were particularly informative because they account for overlap among predictors. Based on this measure, impact was the most influential driver, followed by trust and service. The regression-based approaches led to a very similar conclusion.

Overall, the findings suggest that customers are most satisfied when the card makes a meaningful difference in their lives, comes from a trusted brand, and provides strong customer service. These factors appear to have a larger influence on satisfaction than popularity, rewards, or differentiation from competing cards.
